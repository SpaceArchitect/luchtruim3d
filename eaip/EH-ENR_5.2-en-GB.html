ENR 5.2 MILITARY EXERCISE AND TRAINING AREAS AND AIR DEFENCE IDENTIFICATION ZONE (ADIZ)

1 GENERAL

Airspaces where potential hazards to aircraft operations may exist and all areas over which the operation of civil aircraft may be restricted, are defined in the following categories:
  • Temporary reserved airspaces (TRA);
  • Temporary segregated area (TSA);
  • Cross border areas (CBA);
  • Other permanent hazardous areas.

TRA, TSA and CBA are shown on En-route chart - ICAO in ENR 6.

For some of the above mentioned airspaces/restricted areas, a flight planning buffer zone (FBZ) is created (see ENR 1.10). The FBZ is the associated airspace which may be applied to a reserved/restricted area.

An FBZ is identified by the identification code of the associated restricted area with an added Z. For example, EHTRA10A has FBZ EHTRA10AZ.

Furthermore, some of the published areas may be AMC manageable; see ENR 5.1.

2 TEMPORARY RESERVED AIRSPACE (TRA)

Definition: a defined volume of airspace normally under the jurisdiction of one aviation authority and temporarily reserved, by common agreement, for the specific use by another aviation authority and through which other traffic may be allowed to transit, under ATC clearance.

Certain flying activities are not readily adaptable to air traffic control, since specific aircraft - during at least part of their flight - cannot maintain a constant profile, heading and speed (e.g. test flights, air combat training manoeuvres). Based on the provisions laid down in PANS-ATM (Doc 4444, chapter 16, paragraph 16.1.3), a certain portion of the airspace in the Amsterdam FIR has been designated as a TRA.

The following applies:
  • En route IFR and VFR GAT shall remain clear of the areas.
  • The areas are activated by AUP.
  • Authorisation for use of the areas is subject to pre-scheduling and is granted only to OAT and/or special test flights.
  • MILATCC Schiphol provides ATC service for OAT en-route to/from the areas below FL 245. Maastricht UAC provides ATC service for OAT en-route to/from the areas above FL 245.
  • Air defence stations of the NATO air defence system are responsible for the air defence operations in the areas, unless the areas have been scheduled for autonomous operations.
  • Air defence service in the areas comprises advisory or positive control service in accordance with parent NATO regulations.

5 MILITARY LOW FLYING

Military low flying areas and routes in the Amsterdam FIR are designated for use by military TFC authorised by the appropriate authority to operate below the minimum height as defined in SERA.3105.

Outside low flying areas, aircraft of State Police and military fixed wing aircraft in connection with exercises of the Netherlands Armed Forces may operate below 500 FT AGL/AMSL as authorised by the appropriate authority. Additionally, military helicopters may operate over water areas. 

Note: military low flying routes are listed in ENR 3.3. 

5.1 Low flying areas

Built up areas, populous beaches, crowds, Royal residences, hospitals, health resorts, etc. shall be avoided as far as practicable. Listing of a low flying area does not imply any right to a pilot to use that low flying area.

Note:  the civil and military low flying areas are depicted on the LFR - LFA index chart in ENR 6.
